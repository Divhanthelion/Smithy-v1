# Lateral verification

Read this **before** ingesting an unfamiliar publisher, account, or extraordinary claim. After you have already treated it as evidence is too late.

Vertical reading — the page is coherent, pretty, or self-credentialed — is not verification.

## Four moves

1. **Stop** — do not finish the document. Do not quote it yet.
2. **Investigate the source** — new searches, not the page's About. Who publishes it, who funds it, what they have gotten wrong, whether they own the artifact the claim is about.
3. **Find better coverage** — if the claim is load-bearing, look for independent consensus (or independent contradiction) from owners and specs. One fluent page is not coverage.
4. **Trace original context** — commentary, screenshots, excerpts, and "a study shows" go back to the primary: paper, dataset, filing, commit, unedited source. If the primary does not say what the secondary said, the secondary is not evidence.

## Keep / drop

- Keep: you can name the owner, you fetched the primary, the claim survives a second independent source.
- Drop: unknown publisher you only investigated from its own page; misattributed quote; chart or table you cannot read from the original.
- Closed or proprietary corpora with no external coverage: you cannot SIFT them. Put them in Unknown, or tag `owner` only if this repo / the running system *is* the corpus.

## Failures

- Investigating the author via the same article's bio.
- Treating "lots of similar Google results" as independent coverage when they copy one wire story.
- Using SIFT as a reason to skip reading the primary you already have.
